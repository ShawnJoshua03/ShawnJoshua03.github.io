## The Problem

Fractional ownership lets many people pool money into one rental property. It only works if every investor can see what they own, what it earns and how risky it is, and if the payouts are exactly right.

## What I Built

FRIP, the Fractional Rental Investment Platform. Users register, browse properties with their funding progress, expected returns and monthly income, and invest. Every investment recalculates ownership shares across all investors in that property. An admin action distributes rental income to every shareholder in proportion to their stake.

A dashboard shows each user's allocation as a doughnut chart, their holdings, transaction history, and a time-weighted return on their portfolio. The frontend is a single-page app with client-side routing and no full-page reloads.

## How It Works

<ol class="pipeline">
  <li><b>Auth</b><span>Flask sessions with bcrypt-hashed passwords.</span></li>
  <li><b>Invest</b><span>POST /investments records the amount and recomputes every investor's ownership share for that property.</span></li>
  <li><b>Distribute</b><span>POST /distributions/&lt;property&gt; pays out rental income proportionally and logs a transaction per shareholder.</span></li>
  <li><b>Risk score</b><span>A weighted average of five factors, each from 0 to 1: valuation volatility (25%), investor concentration measured with the Herfindahl index (20%), funding level (20%), cap rate (20%) and depreciation (15%). Scaled to 0 to 100: under 30 is Low, 30 to 55 Medium, above 55 High.</span></li>
  <li class="out"><b>Time-weighted return</b><span>The Modified Dietz method weights each investment by how long it was deployed, so a deposit made yesterday does not distort the return.</span></li>
</ol>

## Results

A working full-stack app with ten API endpoints and five MongoDB collections (users, properties, investments, transactions and valuation history), deployed to Vercel. Risk scores appear on the property list, the property detail page and the portfolio, and the portfolio summary card shows the time-weighted return.
