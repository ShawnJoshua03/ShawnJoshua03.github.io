## The Problem

Modern matching apps compare tags and interest metrics. Two people who both list "hiking" match; two people who would actually get along may not. Hi-Five adds the missing signal: personality.

## What I Built

A matching app built at SASEHack in October 2024 with two teammates. New users take a personality test based on the OCEAN model (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism) and pick interest tags. Hi-Five turns the answers into embeddings, stores everyone in a Neo4j graph, and suggests people with similar personalities and interests. From there users can swipe, send friend requests and chat.

My commits covered the Next.js frontend, login and interest tags, the API endpoints, and chat.

## How It Works

<ol class="pipeline">
  <li><b>Personality test</b><span>One free-text answer per OCEAN trait.</span></li>
  <li><b>Embed</b><span>Each answer is embedded with BAAI/bge-en-icl, giving five trait vectors per person.</span></li>
  <li><b>Graph</b><span>People, tags and friendships live in Neo4j.</span></li>
  <li><b>Score</b><span>For each candidate: the cosine similarity of each trait vector, averaged across the five traits, weighted 0.6, plus the Jaccard similarity of interest tags, weighted 0.4.</span></li>
  <li class="out"><b>Suggest</b><span>The highest-scoring candidates come back as suggested matches.</span></li>
</ol>

## Limitations

<div class="corrections">
<span class="label">Correction</span>

An earlier version of my resume described a professional matching platform that combined embeddings with emotion classification (roberta-base-go-emotions). The public code matches on OCEAN personality embeddings and shared interest tags. It contains no emotion classifier.

</div>

- **Untested weights.** The 0.6 and 0.4 weights are fixed in code. Nothing in the repository measures match quality.
- **A thin personality signal.** Five free-text answers stand in for a validated personality inventory.
- **Hackathon code.** The embedding server address is hard-coded, and the repository README is the default template.

## What I'd Do Next

<ol class="next-list">
  <li><strong>Measure match quality</strong>, even with a small survey of whether suggested matches actually talked, and tune the weights against it.</li>
  <li><strong>Use a validated personality inventory</strong> instead of five free-text prompts.</li>
</ol>
